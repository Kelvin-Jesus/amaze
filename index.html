Amaze

“Amaze.” — Rocky, Project Hail Mary

You let go of the handrail 408 km above the Pacific. Fly where you like — there is nothing out here to push against, so you thrust, and then you keep going. The twenty-seven objects are laid out by their true distance from Earth, which is why the entire solar system fits in the first breath of the scale and the rest of the trip is dark.

Figures are the published values for each object; where astronomers disagree — Betelgeuse’s distance, Stephenson 2-18’s radius — the disagreement is noted rather than hidden. Object list drawn from the Wikipedia lists of astronomical objects.

Five stations are fiction and each wears a dashed tick, because none of them has a distance to put on this axis. Gargantua is the black hole built for Interstellar (2014) out of real Kerr-metric ray tracing. Erid is Rocky’s world from Andy Weir’s Project Hail Mary, parked at the real 40 Eridani A; fly close with the sound on and it speaks to you in chords, the way Eridians do.
Thrust with WASD or the arrow keys, drag to steer, scroll or pinch to zoom. C cycles crew, P adds a second player on the arrow keys (you share one EVA, so you cannot drift out of each other’s sight), F goes fullscreen, [ and ] jump between objects, M cuts the sound. Rocky flies in a xenonite bubble, since twenty-nine atmospheres of ammonia do not travel well. The delta-v counter is vanity — a real SAFER jetpack carries about 3 m/s in total, enough to get you back to the handrail and no further.
The music is generated live in the browser — no recording — and opens up the further out you get. Pixel look and free roam after hallucinate.site.
